Demigod: Chronicles of Olympus

Everything the mod does, and how to survive it.

A total-content Percy Jackson mod for Minecraft. You are claimed by a god, hunted by things that can smell what you are, and sent after monsters by an Oracle that rhymes. This manual documents every mechanic, item, recipe and command in the build — read from source, not from memory.

Minecraft 1.21.11 Loader NeoForge 21.11 Mod ID chronoly Java 21
Installing

Getting it running

There are no dependencies. Drop it in and go.

  1. Install NeoForge for Minecraft 1.21.11

    Any 21.11.x build. The mod is compiled against the newest one at build time.

  2. Put chronoly-0.1.0.jar in your mods folder

    Windows: %appdata%\.minecraft\mods. macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/minecraft/mods.

  3. Launch, make a world, and type /chronoly help

    Everything in this manual is reachable from that one command.

Server play

The mod is server-authoritative — every decision about damage, energy and favour is made on the server, so a modified client cannot cheat any of it. The jar goes on both the server and each client.

Getting started

Your first hour

Nothing needs explaining to you in a menu. The world does the teaching, mostly by hurting you.

  1. A satyr finds you

    Within your first minutes, a satyr falls into step beside you and talks you through four stages. He gives you two drachmas at stage three and a bronze knife at the end. He will not appear again once you're claimed.

  2. Get celestial bronze before you fight anything

    Ordinary iron cannot touch a monster — it passes straight through. Craft a celestial bronze ingot (gold + copper around a blaze rod) and make a dagger or a sword from it.

  3. Kill one monster

    The first monster you kill with a divine weapon claims you. A god is picked for you by weighted random — you do not choose, and the Big Three are 3% combined. From that moment you have a power, a fatal flaw and a scent.

  4. Press G

    Your parent's signature power. It costs Divine Energy, which the HUD shows top-left along with your favour.

  5. Ask the Oracle for a quest

    /chronoly prophecy. You get four lines of verse and 60 real minutes to kill what it names. Finishing pays 120 favour; letting the clock run out costs 40.

Core mechanic

The Mist decides who can hurt whom

This is the most important rule in the mod, and everything else is arranged around it. Celestial bronze passes harmlessly through mortals. Mortal steel passes harmlessly through monsters. You are hurt by both.

Your weaponvs. Monstervs. Mortal / animalvs. Demigod
Divine metal (bronze, imperial gold, Stygian iron)Full damagePhases throughFull damage
Mortal steel (anything else)Phases throughFull damageFull damage
Backbiter (bypasses the Mist)Full damageFull damageFull damage
Fire, falling, drowning, Greek fireFull damageFull damageFull damage

Mortal steel is defined by exclusion, not by a list. A weapon is divine only if it carries the chronoly:divine_weapon tag. Everything else in the game — including swords added by other mods that don't know this mod exists — fails against a monster. That is deliberate: enumerating every mortal weapon would go stale the moment somebody adds a sword.

The first time the rule surprises you, the game says so once, in plain language, and then never mentions it again.

What counts as a monster

Zombies, husks, drowned, skeletons, strays, wither skeletons, spiders, cave spiders, creepers, endermen, witches, blazes, ghasts, magma cubes, phantoms, vindicators, pillagers, ravagers, evokers, hoglins, zoglins, piglin brutes, the Warden, the Wither and the Ender Dragon.

Mortals are villagers, wandering traders, iron golems and every passive animal. Anything the mod doesn't recognise is treated as mortal — so an unknown creature is safe from your bronze, not vulnerable to it.

Parentage

Being claimed

You do not pick your parent. Kill your first monster and a god speaks for you.

Claiming is a weighted roll. The Big Three — Poseidon, Zeus and Hades — are 3% combined, which is roughly what the books imply and is meant to make a child of Poseidon rare enough to be a story. The other five are drawn evenly from the remainder. Your parent fixes three things permanently: your signature power, how your energy regenerates, and which fatal flaw runs in your blood.

The point of the regeneration table

A son of Poseidon in a desert is a different resource than one in the sea, without a single ability being switched off. Where you fight matters as much as what you brought. Alaska — anywhere flagged beyond the gods' reach — zeroes regeneration for everyone.

Ares' and Hermes' rules are invented; the source brief gives no regeneration clause for either, and both are flagged in the code for sign-off.

Progression

Divine Energy and your parent's favour

Energy is a debt, not a wall

Your pool is 100 + favour × 0.4, so growing in your parent's regard genuinely means holding more. Spending past empty is always allowed — the shortfall becomes debt, drawn on the HUD eating into the bar from the right. Debt burns off at 1.5 per second. The books are about people who overreach, so the mod lets you.

Be honest about this table

Fifteen favour reasons are fully specified with tested coefficients in the core model, and nine now fire during play. Boasting, burial rites and Styx oaths still need mechanics of their own; a fake trigger would be worse than an honest gap.

Ambrosia and nectar will kill you

Both heal — ambrosia 10, nectar 6 — and both add to a burn counter that only cools at 0.15 per second. Ambrosia adds 40 per dose, nectar 20. Cross 100 and you start burning from the inside out. A mortal who eats either simply dies: "It tastes like a memory, and then like burning. This was never meant for you."

Fatal flaws

Every flaw cuts both ways

A flaw that is only a downside is a punishment; one that is only an upside is a perk. Each of these is strong enough that you'd choose it and dangerous enough that it will kill you.

Hades' grudge is the only flaw that keeps a ledger: the mod records the entity type of whatever killed you, permanently, and reads it back on every hit you land.

Threat

Scent — why getting stronger is dangerous

Power raises scent, scent raises threat. Getting stronger raises the stakes instead of flattening them, and that loop is load-bearing.

Your scent runs 0 to 1 and is built from four things: your favour (up to 0.50 of it), your parent's rarity (up to 0.25 — a child of Poseidon reeks), the divine relics you're carrying (up to 0.15) and how recently you've killed (up to 0.10).

ScentThreatWhat comes for youCan you hide underground?
0.00 – 0.15UnseenNothing extra.Yes
0.15 – 0.45FaintOccasional attention.Yes
0.45 – 0.75NoticedOne vindicator, husk or stray at a time, ×1.35 health and ×1.25 damage.No
0.75 – 1.00HuntedTwo at a time — ravagers, vindicators, wither skeletons — at ×1.8 health and ×1.6 damage.No
Digging down is not a strategy

The spawn director tests your scent, not the light level. Above 0.45 you cannot hide underground — burying yourself in a hole puts you in a small dark room with the thing that followed you in.

A ward zeroes scent outright, rather than reducing it. That absoluteness is what makes Camp Half-Blood feel like home rather than merely safer.

Monsters

The nine named monsters

Each one is a fight with its own rule, not a bigger health bar — and each now wears its own body: the Minotaur a horned bulk, Medusa a snake-haired figure, Cerberus one dog with three heads, Charybdis a ring of teeth. Two of them cannot be beaten by damage at all.

Son of Pasiphaë

Speed0.34

He charges. Every seven seconds, if you're at running distance, he's abruptly covering it — sidestep or be somewhere else. At half health a horn snaps off in your hands and he gets markedly faster and permanently stronger.

How to fight one

An operator summons them with /chronoly summon <name>, and the Oracle sends you after one when you ask for a prophecy. Each gets a boss bar in its own colour. Killing one is worth +90 favour, or +210 if it was the one the Oracle named.

The five named relics are not craftable. Each comes off the monster that guarded it, which is the only reason to fight the hard ones twice.

Places

Camp Half-Blood, the Underworld, Olympus

Camp Half-Blood

In a fresh world it raises itself a little off spawn within the first ten minutes; in an older world an operator raises it with /chronoly camp build. Twenty cabins go up in the horseshoe the books describe, Zeus and Hera at the head, each in its god's colours with a numbered sign. A ward covers a 48-block radius and zeroes scent completely inside it — monsters stop coming, and a new player can breathe. Check where you stand with /chronoly camp ward.

The Underworld

/chronoly travel underworld. The shore, the gate and all three districts build themselves into the terrain on first arrival — Asphodel is a grey field of white tulips where fourteen silent shades mill about, Elysium has the only grass in the Underworld — gold paths, glowstone hung like a borrowed sky, and six heroes at rest walking it — and Punishment is blackstone, lava channels, chains over iron-barred pens, and five tormentors who are not neutral about visitors. On arrival you are judged. The pavilion reads your whole life and routes you, and it checks for Punishment before Elysium on purpose — a hero who broke an oath on the Styx is not redeemed by the quests they finished first.

VerdictSent becauseWhat it's like
PunishmentYou broke a Styx oath, killed three who couldn't fight back, or committed eight acts of hubrisExactly as advertised
ElysiumFive heroic deeds, or two quests, or 700 peak favourThree lifetimes here unlocks the Isles of the Blest
Asphodel"You were, on balance, fine"Grey, and blinding on arrival

Getting out costs a drachma. Charon does not ferry anybody for nothing. Hold a golden drachma and walk through the gate, or use /chronoly charon. Arrive with empty pockets and you are given exactly one drachma, exactly once, so nobody is stranded forever.

Olympus

/chronoly travel olympus, six hundred floors up. Arrival builds the place if it isn't standing: a circular marble plaza underfoot, a lit walkway, and the hall of thrones at the end of it. Twelve thrones, and eleven of them have nothing for you — being told so by eleven gods in turn is part of the experience of standing in that room.

Petition your own parent with /chronoly olympus <god>. You need 150 favour, and the boon is once per character: your energy is refilled, your debt cleared, and you gain permanent Health Boost II plus Regeneration II. "Something settles into your chest that was not there before. It does not feel like a gift so much as a debt."

Come back with /chronoly travel home.

Quests

The Oracle of Delphi

Prophecies are generated objective-first: a real, solvable quest is planned, and the verse is written as a view onto it. The rhyme never describes something you cannot do.

/chronoly prophecy gives you four lines in two rhyming couplets, naming a target, a place, a relic and a cost — shuffled, so the same quest reads differently each time. Every monster in the mod has its own kennings; the Oracle is structurally unable to name something it has no words for.

The named monster is real. The moment the verse ends, it spawns 90–140 blocks out at a random bearing, and the text tells you which of the eight winds to walk into. The world holds up its end of the contract.

  • Deadline: 72,000 ticks — 60 real minutes, three Minecraft days.
  • Reward: +120 favour on top of the +90 for the boss itself.
  • Failure: −40 favour, and "whatever you were sent for, somebody else will have to go."
  • Check it: /chronoly quest.
Economy

Drachmas, rainbows and two snakes

A golden drachma is two gold nuggets, and it is the only currency the mod has. It pays Charon, it opens an Iris-message, and it pays Hermes Express.

Iris-messaging

/chronoly iris <player>, one drachma. The rainbow requirement is real: you need water within reach, open sky and daylight — all three, or there is no rainbow and no call. When it connects you learn where the other player is, roughly how hurt they are, and what the light is like around them. They see the air in front of them shimmer.

Hermes Express

/chronoly deliver <player>, one drachma. George and Martha carry the parcel and complain the whole way; distance costs time, which is the only honest way to price it. Without a coin: "No coin, no delivery," says Martha. "We are not a charity."

Honest section

What doesn't work yet

This is v0.1.0. Everything above is real and in the jar; everything below is missing, and you should know before you install it.

  • Nothing has been observed running. The build compiles and packages in CI, but no automated test has ever launched the game. The mod's runtime behaviour is verified by reasoning and by 51 unit tests over the Minecraft-free core, not by a server that booted.
  • Boss animation is a first pass. All nine monsters have their own custom-modelled bodies, hand-painted textures, and procedural motion — walking legs, weaving hydra necks, beating wings, snake hair that never stops, a maw that grinds. What they lack is attack animations and bespoke poses; hits land without a lunge to telegraph them.
  • Abilities are one tier of four. Favour tiers change your energy pool and nothing else. Higher tiers are meant to change what an ability is, and they don't yet.
  • Camp auto-builds only in fresh worlds. In a world under ten minutes old, camp raises itself a little off spawn; in an older world it still waits for an operator, because a summer camp landing on somebody's house is not lore, it is griefing.
  • Six of the fifteen favour reasons are not hooked up — down from eleven. Boasting, oaths and burial rites are the notable absences.
  • No in-game guidebook. This page is it.
  • The Labyrinth, charmspeak, and the Fissure of Erebus are designed and partly built in the core model, but have no in-game entry point.
Fixed while writing this manual

Documenting the weapons turned up a real bug: Backbiter, the Hunting Knives and the Electric Spear were missing from the divine-weapon tag, so all three were classified as mortal steel and passed harmlessly through every monster — three of eight weapons, two of them boss-tier, did literally nothing to the things they were made to fight. All three now work, and Backbiter got the third damage axis it always needed.
